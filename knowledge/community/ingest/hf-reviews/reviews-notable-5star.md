### N4 — ElectroMechanician, July 27, 2026 — "I Learned to Weld Aluminum by Accident. Thanks, Vulcan." — 5★ (inferred: no explicit rating tag in this capture line, but page-default sort is "Highest Rating" and content is unreservedly positive)
**Verified buyer:** no tag present
**Helpful:** 1 up / 0 down

The single longest, most load-bearing review in the corpus. Full verbatim text (this is the one file where the complete review, not an excerpt, is warranted given its density of concrete claims):

> There's a whole subculture of guys who have never welded anything more structural than a lawn chair, and who will die on the hill that a machine is worthless unless the badge starts with an L or an M and the price starts with a 3. Same guys with the $700 helmet and $12 of consumables. I bought the Vulcan OmniPro 220. A thousand bucks for the machine. Then I went back for the spool gun. Then the TIG pedal. I've had it about 3 years, and I'm still looking for its ceiling. MIG, flux core, DC TIG and DC stick, all out of one cart. It runs off 120V when you're out in the weeds fixing something that shouldn't have broken, then swaps to 240 for the full 220 amps when you're building something that should never break. 132 preloaded programs on a color screen. You tell it the material and the wire, it does the rest. My SOP: let it dial itself in, then go up one. It guesses honest. Honest runs cold. Synergic settings are cheating and I've made peace with that. A little over a year ago someone brought me a boat with a cracked aluminum hull. I had never welded aluminum in my life. Loaded the spool gun, told the machine what it was looking at, and ran it. Crack closed up clean and it barely took any time at all. I walked away from that boat with a skill I didn't have that morning. It came with the machine. Nobody puts that on the box. On the TIG side, the pedal buys you heat control you'd normally pay triple for. Roll into it, back it down, walk the cup. The arc is smooth. It's the kind of puddle that makes you flip your hood up and stare a second longer than you need to. One thing it won't do, and you should hear it from me instead of the comments. It's DC TIG only. No AC, which means no TIG on aluminum. That's what the spool gun is for. If you were planning on laying pretty stacked dimes across a valve cover, go buy something else. Would've been nice to have AC TIG, but I'm sure I'd have paid a LOT more than a grand if it did! What the haters can't square is that these keep turning up in working shops and on pro fab channels. The machine works. Working is the whole job description. Is it a Miller? No. It's a Vulcan, it costs what it costs, and it lays welds that pass the only inspection that matters. My daughter's exhaust has been on for over 2.5 years. That boat went back in the water and nobody's called me about it since. Unless they drowned, I'll take that as a good sign. Buy the machine. Then buy twice the practice time you think you need. Verdict: best thousand dollars I've ever handed a cashier.

*Notable claims worth surfacing to the agent: 132 preloaded synergic programs; "let it dial itself in, then go up one, it guesses honest" (a MIG-preset-runs-cold correction, the minority view against the dominant "presets run hot" complaint — see themes.md and preset-truth.md for the full disagreement); explicit DC-only TIG limitation stated unprompted by a happy owner, not just critics.*
