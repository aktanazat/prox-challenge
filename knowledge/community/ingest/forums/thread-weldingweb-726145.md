## My Vulcan Omnipro 220 won't arc anymore when MIG welding — resolved by a loose ground

- Source: https://www.weldingweb.com/threads/726145
- Started by JayHanig, Jan 9, 2025 · 10 posts

**OP [JayHanig]:** A buddy and I were working on a small project a couple of days ago and stopped after a short time to do something. We left the welder on; just resting. My Omnipro was doing great during our first session but then it just wouldn't spark when I pulled the trigger after that. This was while MIG welding with 240 volts... I have the setup screen working, it squirts out C25 shielding gas appropriately when I pull the trigger, and it continues to feed wire on demand. Just no spark. This is not an issue of trying to stretch the service cycle more than 25% and overheating. The steel was clean; the ground is clean and tight. I don't know where to look first.

**[SidecarFlip]:** My only comment is, they are 100% solid state IGBT machines and I actually own 3 of them but I also took the extended warranty on all of them... Being 100% solid state, you would have to find an electronics whizz to even diagnose the issue.

**[robert-r]:** & the problem with Harbor Freight welders is (to my knowledge) Harbor Freight doesn't repair them.

**[Bradley256]:** The Vulcan OMNIPRO 220 is a "multiprocess" welder, so does it work in stick or TIG mode? This would tell you if the problem is in the power supply or functions specific to MIG mode.
> **[mhooker32]** exactly. got to try all the modes.

**[Sicon614]:** Make sure the Aux cable is looped into the Stick/TIG receptacle for MIG welding as shown in the inner placard and the connections feeding the male and female ends are tight or the wire will feed, but not arc. Hope this helps.

**[BillE.Dee]:** Break out the manual and go over all of the connections. DON'T just point at them, actually touch them and make certain of connection. Check IF it's working on other processes.

**[MarkVance]:** I hope you have found your fix. With mine, every issue has been a loose connection. As posted previously, physically check everything is tight.

**Resolution — JayHanig, 4 months later:** "I'm embarrassed to admit it but **the ground cable that I was certain was tight actually wasn't**. A dumb mistake, but ultimately all it cost me was some time, aggravation, and some of my pride."

> **[SusKatCas]** It's a journey. Odds are you'll do it again, but find it sooner next time.

**Takeaway:** wire feeds, gas flows, and the setup screen looks fine — but no arc — points first at the ground/electrical-path connections (loose ground clamp, or the Aux cable not fully seated in the Stick/TIG receptacle that MIG mode routes through on this machine), not the power supply itself. The OP had already re-checked "clean and tight" by eye and still missed it; the community's advice was consistent: physically tug/touch every connection, don't just look at it.
