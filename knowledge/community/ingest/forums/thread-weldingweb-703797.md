## Vulcan Omnipro 220 TIG problems — pulsating arc, resolved by a helmet

- Source: https://www.weldingweb.com/threads/703797
- Started by pwspringer, Jan 3, 2019 · 14 posts

**OP [pwspringer]:** Our shop recently purchased the Omnipro 220 and have had good luck with it except I can't seem to get the TIG to work the way it should. I called Harbor Freight and tried all of their suggestions... Got the replacement and I am still having the same issue. We purchased the optional foot pedal too... Here is what it is doing, after starting the arc it won't maintain a steady arc. It seems to pulsate erratically. The only way I can get it to not pulsate is [to] turn the amps way down, then it's too low to even form a puddle.

The diagnostic exchange that followed is the load-bearing part of this thread — a step-by-step elimination of every plausible TIG cause before the real one turned up:

> **[Louie1961]** What gas are you using for TIG? (should be pure argon, not C25....you can't use MIG gas), what flow rate? what kind and size of tungsten? what kind of metal are you trying to weld? How is the tungsten ground/prepared?
>> **[pwspringer]** I am using 100% Argon at a flow rate of 20 cfh. I am using 3/32" 2% ceriated tungsten, from Harbor Freight, ground to about 60 degree point. I have tried welding 16ga steel and stainless steel, no difference.

> **[jerje]** I don't own one but does it have a pulse setting feature like my Lincoln SW200 Tig... Is it set to pulse?
>> **[pwspringer]** No pulse setting that I am aware of.

> **[filetobeef]** do you have correct polarity?
> **[N2 Welding]** And did you leave the wire feed power disconnected as per quick start up guide?
> **[CAVEMANN]** Are you sure the bottle has what it says it has, mislabels and bad gas do happen.

**[pat h]:** 95% of the time I had problems with welders it was something I did or forgot to do... your settings seem fine as long as polarity is correct and a good ground.

**[pwspringer]:** Thanks for all the replies. Yes, I'm sure the gas is Argon... The polarity is correct. I even switched it just to try and it burned up the tip. I'm curious about leaving the wire feed power disconnected, I'll have to check when I get back to work Monday.

**Resolution — pwspringer, 11 days later:** "I wanted to follow up with the problem I was having with the pulsating TIG, it has been resolved. I'm almost too embarrassed to say what the problem was but thought it may help someone down the road. **The problem wasn't with the welder, it was cause[d] by a faulty auto-darkening welding helmet. I grabbed my 30 year old fixed lens helmet and the problem was gone.**"

> **[N2 Welding]** Oh wow now that's funny...

**Takeaway for diagnostics:** every classic TIG-arc-instability cause (gas purity/flow, tungsten prep, polarity, ground, pulse setting) was checked and ruled out over 12 posts before the actual fault — a malfunctioning auto-darkening lens flickering the operator's view and making a stable arc *look* like it's pulsating — was found. Worth surfacing "what are you viewing the arc through?" as a diagnostic question, not just machine settings.
