## Harbor Freight Vulcan Omnipro 220 stick rod review

- Source: https://www.weldingsite.com/threads/harbor-freight-vulcan-omnipro-220-stick-rod-review.301/
- 5 posts, all by Gary Fowler except the last two replies

**OP [Gary Fowler]:** I have had this machine for a couple months or more but just got around to checking out the stick rod function. It does have a setting for 6010 and one for 7018 so I first tried it with 6010. I set it up for 1/8 and 1/4" plate which is what I was welding on. I struck an arc and immediately thought I had the leads reverse[d] and it was on straight polarity. I just swapped the leads without looking at the terminals and WOW, it really started frying with that. I went back, checked the + and - on the machine, switched the leads back where they were and lowered the amps from 135 down to 125 then down to 100. This was a bit more acceptable but the arc blow was still significant with the 6010. I next set it up for 1/8" 70xx per the machine program. It welded a bit better with 7018 but the arc still didn[']t work for me so I shut it down, unspooled my Miller Dialarc 250 [and] set it on 125 amps and did my welding, the arc was super smooth just like it should be. **I guess the Vulcan will just be doing wire feed which it does very well for FCAW and MIG.**

**[Gary Fowler], follow-up:** I got out the old TIG torch and hooked it to the Vulcan for repairing my friend[']s luggage rack on his old Honda 300 4x4 and it worked really well. My Miller will stick better but the TIG seemed to be a really smooth arc. So I now have tried it with 6010, 7018, 6013, FCAW, MIG and TIG and all works as they should.

**[Gary Fowler], follow-up:** My friend had a little more mods to do to his 4 wheeler luggage rack. I again rolled out the Vulcan Omni-pro 220 but this time used the MIG with .030 wire and C25 gas. It worked really well on the thin expanded metal and the light wall tubing... I also used the Vulcan a few days ago to weld on some 16 gauge sheet metal to my trailer loading ramps.

**Resolution:** unresolved / thread abandoned. The generator question — a recurring, largely unanswered theme across this whole corpus (see wisdom.md) — gets asked directly here too and never gets a reply in this capture.

**Takeaway:** the one polarity/arc-blow diagnostic in this thread is directly useful — Gary Fowler's stick arc was rough on both 6010 (arc blow, even after backing amps down from 135→100) and 7018, but he initially suspected reversed leads because the arc "immediately" felt wrong; checking + and - on the machine terminals (not just at the electrode holder) is the first move before assuming the machine itself is bad. His overall verdict after trying all five processes (6010, 7018, 6013, FCAW, MIG, TIG): stick arc quality lagged his old Miller Dialarc 250, but MIG/FCAW/TIG all "work as they should."
