## Vulcan Omnipro 220..anyone use it — the duty-cycle argument, then a purchase and pro-technique tips

- Source: https://nc4x4.com/forum/threads/vulcan-omnipro-220-anyone-use-it.195882/
- Started by marty79, Dec 17, 2019 · 61 posts, spanning Dec 2019 to a May 2023 revival

**OP [marty79]:** Looking for any personal reviews on this unit if anyone has used it. I'm not crazy about the newer digital welder but some have better reviews than others... Any other recommendations for good shop 220 welder to handle regular shop usage (like full time usage) thanks.

**[tsconver], early owner:** I have one. It is a beast for mig. It does 7000 series rod pretty good. Have not tried tig. It cannot run aluminum tig. It is tap and go. I have ran three tanks of gas and probably 4 spools of wire through mine.

**The duty-cycle fight — the thread's real content.** A working professional welder posts the machine's actual spec math and calls it hobby-grade:

> **[WARRIORWELDING]:** That be scratch or lift arc start....without checking this sounds like a machine without high frequency a.c. for aluminum gtaw. [posts the duty-cycle chart] These are the important numbers. Also proves the duty cycle is for hobby use at best.
>
> Later, with the actual numbers spelled out: **"Gmaw 19 volts at 40%. Since it requires dang near all that output for short circuit with .035 wire is say she's pretty part time. 4 minutes of good output out of ten. Of course lighter wire will run at lower settings with a bit better duty cycle. Anything heavier th[a]n 11ga. for an extended period running high quality beads without large puddle manipulation will be a struggle."**

> **[tsconver]**, pushing back: Yes, it does not have high frequency. **I disagree with hobby use. I have welded with it all day long and not had any issues.** ... Is it a professional machine for nonstop welding for hours[?] no. **Is it capable of welding axles and cages where you are not welding beads non stop for hours in my opinion definitely.** ... For cost and warranty I would go the omnipro hand[s] down[s].

> **[Fabrik8]**, siding with caution on repairability rather than duty cycle: I struggle with [the] logic of buying something that costs $1k but has very sketchy prospects of being repaired because the company now carries a different brand or uses a different contract manufacturer in China.

> **[hurt4x4]**: If anything happens to it and you purchase the three year warranty, you take it back to your local HF and you get a new one. No down time waiting on repair. The only negative I see is long term parts availability since HF seems to change brands so often.

**The arbitration that actually resolved the argument:**
> **[awheelterd]:** I think he was referring to how often you hit duty cycle, not w[h]ether you're laying bubble gum or dime stacks.
> **[Jody Treadway]** (of Welding Tips and Tricks): All I'll say is HF isn't the place to buy a welder if you're doing professional work... based on the definition of professional. Being paid for the job. **Hobbyist use is fine. Just as @WARRIORWELDING stated, it requires someone to monitor duty cycle and adjust use accordingly. That's not a spray and pray machine.**

The debate escalated into a genuinely heated appearance-vs-substance argument (kaiser715: a shop with an HF welder as its main machine would make him "turn around and leave"; Mac5005 firing back that judging a welder by machine brand rather than weld quality is nonsense) that doesn't add new diagnostic content and isn't reproduced verbatim here.

**Practical technique from Mac5005**, an experienced GMAW user, once the brand argument cooled:

> A quality short circuit gmaw weld depends way more on the weldor behind the helmet, not the welder... I don't really give duty cycle much consideration for gmaw. As long as it's 40% or so at 135 amps or so, let it rip. You aren't a production facility, and you will be better off spacing your welds, with a break between each one, to reset your positioning... The omnipro 200/220 whatever the number is a near exact if not exact clone/copy of the Lincoln MP210.

**Resolution — marty79 bought one** (post #54, ~1 month later): "Well I got blessed with this monster machine. This thing is a beast. Little complex to fine tune but playing with the settings I'm getting there."

**Mac5005's follow-up settings advice — the load-bearing pro technique from this thread:**

> There are two short circuit mig settings. One is CV, the other CC. Learn how to use each for different things and it will make you very happy... **One of the coolest things I did, was get the amperage around 105, then turn the wfs down to slow everything down. Allowed me to make much slower, more easily controlled welds on critical items, without sacrificing my amperage below 100.** ...It's been a few years, but IIRC, welded sch 80 6" pipe, settings were around 100–105 amps, volts @ 17.5, wfs 175–180 with .035" solid. 75/25 mix. Pipe was beveled, gapped, tacked. Welded with 1 root, 1 hot, and 3 stringer cover passes. Passed visual, and bend testing and was flawless... **The settings won't band aid poor technique. The settings will allow you to tailor a good technique to how fast/slow you like to work.**

**2023 thread revival** — Knick313 defends the OmniPro against name-brand alternatives: "The rebel is junk for mig welding... The mig works just fine if you know how to play the duty cycle game and tigging isn't bad, and for those saying you can't tig weld aluminum, you can't run pretty ass instagram welds but it does weld it." marty79 closes the thread out explaining he's since moved through "several Lincoln machines" and settled on a Harbor Freight Titanium 170 specifically for the no-questions-asked in-store exchange warranty: "when you do fab work full time for a living, I can't afford to have a machine go down and be stuck without one... 1.4yrs now welding on this Titanium 170 and I have absolutely 0 complaints other than the 8ft lead."

**Takeaway:** the thread's real value is the WARRIORWELDING/tsconver/Jody Treadway exchange, which is the most precise, professionally-sourced duty-cycle number in the whole corpus (4 min good output out of 10 at 19V/40%/.035" wire) plus the community's actual consensus on what to do with it: it's a real limit that a hobbyist has to monitor and pace around, not a defect, and not a reason to avoid the machine for cage/axle-grade work — just don't run it like a production MIG. Mac5005's CV/CC-mode and "back the amps off, slow the WFS down" technique is a genuinely transferable pro tip independent of brand.
